How to measure a part for a replacement

Eight steps to photograph and measure a broken or discontinued part so it can be modeled and manufactured. No special tools needed, though digital calipers help.

  1. Gather what you have

    The original part is the best reference, even broken. You'll also want:

    • Digital calipers if you have them, or a ruler with millimeter marks
    • Your phone camera
    • The make and model of the equipment the part came from

  2. Photograph it with a scale

    Lay the part on a plain, light background with a ruler beside it. Shoot straight on from the front, side, and top, plus a close-up of anything that clips, threads, or slides into something else.

  3. Measure the critical fits

    Critical fits are the surfaces that touch another part: hose cuffs, shafts, holes, slots, and snap clips. Measure each one three times and write down the average.

    • Use the outside jaws for outside diameters (OD)
    • Use the inside jaws for holes and inside diameters (ID)
    • Don't squeeze soft or rubbery parts; touch the jaws lightly

  4. Measure hole spacing

    For mounting holes, measure center to center. The easy way is to measure edge to edge across two holes, then add one hole diameter.

  5. Get the overall size and thickness

    Measure the overall length, width, and height, and the thickness of walls and tabs. These don't need to be exact, but they help us check the model.

  6. Measure what it connects to

    If the part plugs into something, measure that too: the port, the hose end, or the rail. For dust adapters, measure the outside of the hose cuff and the inside or outside of the tool port. Check the hose size finder for published sizes.

  7. Note how it's used

    Tell us where the part lives and what it does: indoors or outdoors, heat, load, how many you need, and where the original failed. Not sure which material fits? Try the material picker.

  8. Send it to us

    Attach your photos and measurements to a quote request. If something's missing, we'll ask. If the finished part doesn't fit, we reprint it free.
