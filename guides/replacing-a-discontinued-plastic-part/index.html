How to replace a discontinued plastic part

Five ways to find or replace a plastic part the manufacturer no longer sells, from tracking the part number to having it remade.

  1. 1. Find the exact part number
  2. 2. Check for a newer replacement
  3. 3. Look for used or surplus parts
  4. 4. Consider a compatible aftermarket part
  5. 5. Have it remade from the original
  6. When not to replace it yourself

1. Find the exact part number

Look for a number molded into the part, or find it in the parts diagram in the owner's manual or on the maker's website. Search with the equipment's model number, not just the brand. A part number is the fastest way to find a replacement, and it is the first thing a parts counter or a fabricator will ask for.

2. Check for a newer replacement

Manufacturers often replace an old part number with a new one (a "superseded" part) that fits the same machine. A dealer or authorized parts distributor can look this up. If the part is only sold inside a large, expensive assembly, ask whether the small piece is sold separately.

3. Look for used or surplus parts

Online marketplaces, repair shops, and salvage dealers often have parts from retired machines. Check the part number and condition carefully. Our own eBay store (opens in a new tab) lists some ready-made parts.

4. Consider a compatible aftermarket part

An aftermarket part is made by another company to fit the same equipment. For plastic parts such as adapters, clips, covers, caps, nozzles, and rollers, a well-made aftermarket part can match the original and is sometimes stronger where the original failed. Browse our compatible parts catalog by brand or part number.

5. Have it remade from the original

When there is no replacement at all, a broken part can be measured and rebuilt in CAD, then made in a material suited to the job. This is called reverse engineering. Send the original part, even in pieces, or clear photos with a ruler in the frame and the equipment's model number. The measuring guide shows what to send.

When not to replace it yourself

Some parts should only be replaced with genuine, certified parts: anything safety-critical, pressurized, load-bearing, electrical, or part of a braking or steering system, and anything that touches food or is used for medical or child-safety purposes. We don't make those parts. See what we won't make.
